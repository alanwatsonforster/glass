\section{Dassault Ouragan}

\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{Ouragan.jpg}

The Dassault Ouragan was fighter-bomber and the first French-designed jet-powered combat aircraft to be manufactured. Like many of its contemporaries, it had a single engine, an air-intake in its nose, and swept wings and tail. Its name means “hurricane” in French.

\subsection{Versions}

\subsubsection{MD 450A and MD450B}

The initial 450A version used a Rolls-Royce Nene 102 engine. The subsequent 450A version used a Rolls-Royce Nene 104 license-built by Hispano-Suiza engine and had a revised undercarriage. However, it was largely identical to the MD 450A.

It entered service with the Armee de l'Air in 1952, replacing Vampires and other older aircraft. Its service-life was short, and in 1955 it was replaced by the Mystère IV.

The Ouragan also served with the Indian Air Force from 1953 and was known as the Toofani (“hurricane” in Hindi). It was withdrawn from front-line services in 1965.

It also served with the Israeli Air Force from 1953 and served until the 1970s. In 1975, several were sold on to El Salvador where they served until the late 1980s.

\subsection{Armament and Stores}

The principal armament was four 20 mm Hispano-Suiza cannons under the nose. The Ouragan could also carry bombs, napalm tanks, 105 mm Brant T-10 rockets, and SNEB 60~mm rocket pods.

\subsection{Combat}

The Ouragan saw combat with the Israeli Air Force in the Suez Crisis and the 1967 War. In Indian service, it saw combat in the 1962 Sino-Indian War and the 1965 India-Pakistan War.
