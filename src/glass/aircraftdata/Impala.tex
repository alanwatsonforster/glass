\section{Atlas Impala}

The Atlas Impala is a trainer and light attack aircraft manufactured by the Atlas Aircraft Corporation of South Africa.

The Impala Mk I is a license-built version of the two-seat Aermacchi MB-326M.

The Impala Mk II is a license-built version of the single-seat Aermacchi MB-326K, albeit with the less powerful Viper 20-540 engine previously used in the Impala Mk I rather than the more powerful Viper 632-43 of the Aermacchi-built MB-326K.

Both versions served with the South African Air Force. Some Mk IIs were later sold to the Brazilian FAB where they were redesignated as AT-26A.

In the SAAF, they saw combat in the South African Border War, typically armed with up to six Matra F2 rocket pods (each with six 68 mm rockets) or with 120 or 250 kg (250 and 500 lb) bombs and sometimes also equipped with a photographic reconnaissance pod or 330L fuel tanks. Although the outer pylons were apparently wired for IRMs, there is no evidence that they were carried. They flew both daylight and nighttime {\itshape Maanskyn} (moonshine) missions.
